## 📌 Stage 1: Project Overview

Stage 1 focused on selecting the project topic, **“Survey Lung Cancer”**, and collecting relevant data from publicly available sources. The dataset was organized into the following components:

- **Raw Data**
- **Pre-processed / Integrated Data**
- **Basic Group Report**

---

### 📝 Project Introduction (Excerpt)

Lung cancer is projected to affect **14,529 individuals in Australia by the end of 2022**, making it a significant public health concern. While established risk factors such as smoking and environmental pollution are well documented, there is a need for continued examination of the **mental and physical symptoms** associated with lung cancer to support early detection and prevention.

Understanding these symptoms can help identify the disease at earlier stages, reducing progression to more severe conditions. Additionally, research indicates that lung cancer may be **hereditary**, highlighting the importance of preventative measures for future generations.

This study aims to examine lung cancer symptoms influenced by **social, environmental, mental, and physical factors**, providing deeper insight into early-stage characteristics of the disease.

---

### 🎯 Key Stakeholders
- Young people (particularly with the rise of vaping)
- Individuals with a family history of smoking or lung cancer
- Healthcare professionals
- Government bodies
- Educational institutions

By improving awareness and recognition of lung cancer symptoms, this research supports early intervention, informed healthcare decisions, and targeted education initiatives to reduce long-term risk.

## 📌 Stage 2: Data Analysis and Reporting

Stage 2 focused on conducting a **data-driven investigation** around a single, shared topic of interest relevant to identified stakeholders. Building on Stage 1, the group continued exploring the **lung cancer survey topic**, using cleaned and integrated datasets from Stage 1, with the option to incorporate additional public datasets where relevant.

---

### 🔍 Topic and Datasets
All group members worked on the same overarching research question, examining relationships within the data rather than simple factual comparisons. Each member was free to analyse different datasets or different aspects of the issue, provided their work contributed meaningful insights for stakeholders such as healthcare professionals, policymakers, and the wider community.

---

### 📊 Data Summaries and Visualisations
Each member:
- Produced one or more **grouped-aggregate summaries** using Python
- Generated one or more **distinct charts** to visualise relationships in the data
- Ensured all summaries and charts were unique across the group

These analyses focused on identifying patterns, trends, or potential relationships within the data rather than drawing definitive conclusions.

---

### 🧑‍💻 Individual Analysis (Part A)
Individually, members:
- Used **Python** to compute grouped summaries and present them as tables
- Created charts using Python or spreadsheet tools
- Documented how each chart was produced
- Evaluated the effectiveness of each visualisation using principles discussed in lectures (data attributes vs. visual encodings)

Each member’s work was documented in a dedicated section of **Part A** of the report.

---

### 📈 Group Interpretation (Part B)
As a group, members collaboratively wrote **Part B** of the report, aimed at non-technical readers interested in the topic. This section:
- Explained the research question and its relevance
- Highlighted key trends, relationships, or lack thereof
- Supported claims with selected tables and charts from Part A
- Honestly reflected on expectations versus observed results

---

### 📝 Deliverables
Stage 2 required two submissions:
1. A **PDF report** containing:
   - A brief introduction
   - Individual analyses (Part A)
   - A combined interpretation and conclusions (Part B)
2. A **compressed folder** containing:
   - Datasets used by each member
   - Python code and/or spreadsheets for summaries and charts
   - Data provenance, licensing information, and attribute descriptions

<br>

Both submissions were made by one group member on behalf of the entire group, with all members receiving the same mark.

---

Stage 2 emphasized not only technical data analysis skills, but also **clear communication of insights**, coordination across group members, and responsible handling and documentation of data.

## 🤖 Stage 3: Predictive Modelling and Machine Learning

Stage 3 focused on applying **machine learning techniques** to build and evaluate predictive models for a selected attribute within a shared dataset. The goal of this stage was to compare different modelling approaches and assess their effectiveness in making predictions relevant to the chosen domain.

---

### 🎯 Prediction Target and Dataset
As a group, a single **target attribute** (quantitative or nominal) was identified for prediction, along with an appropriate dataset containing this attribute. The group jointly defined the **measure of success** used to evaluate model performance and justified its suitability for the prediction task.

The dataset was split into **training** and **test** sets, with at least **10% reserved for testing**, ensuring fair and unbiased evaluation of all models.

---

### 🧠 Model Development
Group members coordinated to select **distinct machine learning methods**, avoiding duplication and enabling meaningful comparison across approaches. Each member then individually:
- Used **Python** (e.g. `scikit-learn, matplotlib, pandas`) to train a predictive model on the training data
- Tuned hyperparameters using only the training dataset
- Applied any necessary data preprocessing (e.g. scaling or encoding)

The test dataset was strictly reserved for final evaluation and was not used during training or tuning.

---

### 📊 Model Evaluation (Part A)
Individually, each member:
- Evaluated their model using the agreed success metric on the test data
- Reported the evaluation results and discussed model performance
- Documented their methodology, code, and results in their own section of **Part A** of the report

---

### 📘 Comparative Analysis (Part B)
Part B of the report was written collaboratively and targeted readers with an interest in **machine learning**. This section:
- Compared the different predictive models produced by the group
- Discussed strengths, weaknesses, and trade-offs between approaches
- Drew conclusions about which methods performed best and why

---

### 📄 Deliverables
Stage 3 required two submissions:
1. A **PDF report** containing:
   - An overview of the dataset, prediction target, and data split
   - Individual modelling and evaluation sections (Part A)
   - A comparative discussion of models (Part B)
2. A **compressed folder** containing:
   - Datasets used
   - Python notebooks for machine learning/predictive model training, tuning, and evaluation
   - Any metadata or supporting files

Both submissions were made by one group member on behalf of the entire group, with all members receiving the same mark.

---

Stage 3 emphasized **predictive accuracy**, **methodological rigor**, and **clear communication** of machine learning concepts and results.
